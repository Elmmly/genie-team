# Content Editing Conventions

When revising user-authored prose (docs, marketing copy, README text, blog
posts, UI strings), the user's writing is the material — not a first draft to
replace.

## Preserve the author's framing

- Refine the **existing** framing, vocabulary, and voice rather than
  substituting your own. If the user built the piece around a metaphor,
  structure, or recurring phrase, improvements happen inside that frame.
- If you believe the framing itself is the problem, say so explicitly and
  propose the alternative separately — don't ship a rewrite that quietly
  abandons what the user wrote.

## Claims about the text must be quotable

- Never claim a motif, pattern, or "refrain" exists in the text without
  quoting the specific lines that establish it. If you can't quote it, it
  isn't there — and editing as if it were overstates the piece and misleads
  the author about their own writing.
- The same applies to claims like "this echoes the intro" or "consistent with
  the tone elsewhere": cite the passage.

## Typos and apparent errors

- Flag suspected typos, wrong names, or factual slips and **ask** — do not
  silently correct. What looks like a typo may be deliberate (a coined term,
  dialect, an in-joke, a product name).
- Mechanical normalization (double spaces, obvious spelling of common words)
  may be applied directly, but list every such change so the author can veto.

## Scope discipline

- Edit what was asked. A request to "tighten the second paragraph" is not a
  license to restyle the whole document.
- When asked for a light pass, return a light pass — offer the deeper
  rewrite as an option, not as the deliverable.
